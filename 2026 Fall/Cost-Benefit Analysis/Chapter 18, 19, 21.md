### Injections
- Injections (J)
	- Comes from NON HOUSEHOLD entities spending money on final goods and services, injection sources include:
		- Investments (I)

### Injections
- These Injections are assumed to be wholly independent from the flow of income or output. This means they will not be affected by the level of national income.
- Planned injections are the amount of intended spending within a given period
	- Increase in injection DOES increase the total/final

### Withdrawals
- Withdrawals (W)
	- Income earned by HOUSEHOLDS that is not spent on final goods or services within this economy
- Savings (S)
	- Income that is saved, it could go into a savings account, pension, social security, 401K, or portfolio
- Taxation (T)
	- Is revenue taken from BOTH firms and households by the government before it can be spent within the circular flow. This includes state and federal taxes 
- Unlike injections, withdrawals are directly impacted by the current economy as when income increase or decreases it will be reallocated. 

$$
\text{Planned Injections} (I+G+X)=\text{Planned Withdrawals} (S+T+M)
$$

### Getting to Equilibrium: The Adjustment Process
- Say a product is in low demand within the economy and it is not conversely exported
- IT will then be counted as an unexpected increase in stock, which can be counted as unplanned investment so that the subsequent production can be offset (actual investment = planned + unplanned = planned withdrawals)
- Consequently, in the next period, the "firm" will have to cut back on production, due to the backlog of product as well as the recently decrease demand
- This will not effect the initially lowered amount of planned injections as they will continue to be external.
Inversely,
- If there is too much demand from sectors outside of the households and is beyond what output is available. 
- Firms will destock (burn though more stock than planned within the given period)

In both cases, equilibrium is achieved by businesses increasing and decreasing output income release

### Aggregate Demand Movement
- Increase in aggregate demand can increase national income

$$
\text{Marginal propensity to consume domestically}=\frac{\text{Change in consumer spending domestically}}{\text{Change in income}}
$$

If the MPCD was 0.8, that means 80% of the spending is spent on domestic spending.
$$
\frac{1}{1-MPCD}=\frac{1}{1-0.8}=\frac{1}{0.2}=5
$$
So a government spending of 100$ would eventually be 500$ ($100\times 5=500$)

Higher MPCD = larger multiplier, multiplier is smaller in open economy.

### Limitation of real GDP per capita as a measure of a Country's Standard of living

		- Technology makes products cheaper and better (like laptops or TVs)
