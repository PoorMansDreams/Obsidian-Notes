
### Keys to Understand Elasticity of Demand
- The sign of the answer
	- Negative answer: change in quantity demanded and change in variable move in opposite directions
	- Pos answer: variable and quantity move in the same direction - both increase or both decrease 
- The size or value of the answer
	- >1: quantity demanded changed more than variable percentages, so demand is elastic
	- <1: change in quantity demanded is less than change in variable, so demand is inelastic 

- **The Price Elasticity of Demand**
	- Tells you how much people change the amount they buy when the price goes up or down
		- If your usual meal at a fast food place went from 8 to 12, would you still order as often?
		- If the newest phone model costs 200 more than last year's version, would yo upgrade or wait?

$$
\text{Price elasticitiy of demand}= \frac{\text{Change in the quantity demanded}}{\text{change in price}}\times \frac{\text{original price}}{\text{ original quantity demanded}}
$$

$$
\text{Price elasticitiy of demand}= \frac{\text{Change in Quantity Demanded \%}}{\text{Change in Price of Product \%}}
$$
- The factors that people decide whether people continues buying something when the price goes up or immediately switch to something else. 
	- Do people need the product or is it just something they want
	- Are they easy alternatives they can switch to?
	- Does the price change make a big difference in their spending?
	- Are people loyal to the brand or flexible?

### Income Elasticity of Demand
- Income Elasticity of demand measures how demand changes when consumers income changes
- Formula:
$$
 \text{Income elasticity} = \frac{\text{\% change in quantity demanded}}{\text{\% change in income }}  
$$

- If the Value of income is > 1, the product is known as a luxury product causing the demand to be very sensitive to one's income
- As income increases, demand increases by a large percentage
- If the value is < 1, then the product is known as a necessity: demand is not particularly sensitive to income.

### Cross Price Elasticity of Demand: substitutes and complements
- Positive result: Substitutes
	- Products that can replace eachother
	- Price of one goes up means demand for the other goes up (PS4 up $\to$ Demand Xbox up )
- Negative results: Complements
	- Products that are used together
	- price of one going up means demand for the other goes down (PC $\to$ Mouse/Keyboard)

### Demand Elasticity
- Inelastic: people will still buy it even if the price changes
- elastic: demand changes when the price changes.

### What is Supply
- Supply is the product amount the producer is willing to make in a given time period
	- Depends on price, cost, number of producers, resources available
- **Supply Curve**
	- Price = y axis
	- Quantity = x axis
- This curve is usually a upward graph 

### Shifts in Supply
- Increase in Supply, Shift supply curves to the right
- Decrease in Supply, Shifts Supply curve to the left
	- Change # of producers
	- Change of technology
	- change in cost
	- change in indirect taxes
	- change in weather conditions 

### Industry Supply
- Total quantity supplied by all firms in the industry
- The industry supply curve combines the supply decisions of every induvial producer
- Ex: Shows the quantity from all firms being added horizontally 

### Joint Supply
- Source branches out into multiple resources
- Cause and effect situation
- Multiple sectors can be affected resulting in price and demand change 

### The price Elasticity of Supply
$$
\text{ Price elasticity of supply} = \frac{\text{Percentage change in the quantity supplied}}{\text{Percentage change in price}}
$$

- The sign of the answer should be: positive, assuming that the supply curve is upward sloping
- Additionally, An increase in price increases the quantity supplied.
- A fall in the price reduces the quantity supplied 

- If the answer is >1
	- % change in the quantity supplied >% change in supply. Supply is price elastic
- If the answer is <1
	- % change in the quantity supplied is less than the change in price. Supply is price inelastic. 

### Value of the Price Elasticity of Supply
- Number of firms in the industry
	- Allows for easier change of product output responding to price changes
- Time
	- More mastering, more resourcing, more businesses = more price elasticity for supply

### Markets
- A market occurs when buyers and sellers interact to exchange goods and services. THis can be a physical market like a farmers market in which local producers sell their goods. Or it could be an online market, like ebay where buyers and sellers never physically meet each other
- The market can take multiple forms:
	- A local one, regional buyers and sellers such as a taxi business
	- A national one 

### Equilibrium
- Equilibrium occurs in a market when, at the given price the quantity supplies equals the quantity demanded and there is no positive reason for this position to change 
- In a free market, the equilibrium output is reached by the change

### Excess Supply
- Surplus; The price is above the equilibrium level
- A large supply pushes down the price
