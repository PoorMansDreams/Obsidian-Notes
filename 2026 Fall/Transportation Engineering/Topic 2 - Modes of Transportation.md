### Human Sensory Process
- Drivers’ decisions and actions depends on information received through the senses
- Senses in order of importance for driving
	- Visual (sight)
		- • 90% of drivers’ information from vision
		- Vision cone
		- Experienced drivers move head and eye to get more information using the most acute vision
		- Vision acuity declines with age advancing
	- Kinesthetic (movement)
		- Sense of movement
		- Longitudinal acceleration and deceleration
		- Lateral acceleration and deceleration
		- Feedback to drivers’ actions
		- Pressing brake/accelerator
		- Turning steering wheel
	- Vestibular (equilibrium, change of velocity)
	- Auditory (hearing)
		- Alerts from sounds of horn or skidding tire
		- Engine noise for judging vehicle speeds
		- Noise intentionally designed to remind drivers
		- Special pavement texture
		- Raised markers
	- Miscellaneous
		- Smell
		- Temperature
		- Hunger
		- Thirst
		- Fatigue

### Driver Perception and Reaction
- Perception-Reaction time:
	- The time interval between the person seeing/feeling/hearing the traffic situation and starts making a response (starts reacting)
- An example: simple braking situation 
	- The clock starts when the object is visible, and stops when the driver's foot hits the brake

### PIEV
- P (Perception): the process of forming a mental image based on information received from senses
- I (Intellection): reasoning the mental image using intellect
- E (Emotion): driver decides how to react under influence of emotion
- V (Volition): the act of making a choice or decision

### Four steps of information process
- Now generally accepted by highway engineers
	- Four steps:
		- Detection: Seeing the object
		- Identification: Gaining sufficient information
		- Decision: Making a decision
		- Response: Carrying out the action

For a vehicles to move it will have to resist 5 characteristics
- Inertial resistance
	- Resist acceleration (trying to slow down from high speeds $F=ma$)
- Grade resistance
	- The component of the gravitational force acting down a frictionless include surface 
	- Higher Gradation % = steeper = harder to overcome
- Rolling resistance
	- As vehicle tires roll over highway surfaces it still has to fight against the friction from the surface 
- Curve Resistance
	- The force required to cause vehicle to move along a curved path (centripetal force)
- Air Resistance

### Aircraft Trends
- Weight: continuing growth from 1930 to present
	- Note: due to elaborate landing gears, there is no similar increased thickness of airfield pavements
- Fuselage length: grew steadily since 1945
	- Note: aircraft most likely will approach a ceiling on length as designs with multiple decks are introduced
- Wing span: has increased only slightly
	- Note: great news, as aprons, hangars, etc. are slightly affected
	- Cruising speed: 190 mph (1930’s) to 1,800 mph (present)
	- Note: increased speed gives steadily decreasing marginal returns in savings of travel time (benefits vs. costs)

- Runway length: due to turbofan engine, the trend of increased runway length might stop
- Passenger capacity: increase significantly
