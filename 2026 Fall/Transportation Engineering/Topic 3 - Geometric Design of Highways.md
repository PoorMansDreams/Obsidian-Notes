### Design Speed
- A selected speed to determine various geometric design features of the roadway
- An engineer should select a design speed appropriate to assure that drivers can travel at their desired speeds, other factors include
	- Terrain, traffic volume, traffic composition, costs of right-of-way and construction, aesthetic consideration
- Ranging from 15 to 75 mph in increments of 5 mph (15, 20, …, 70, 75 mph). Change in design speed should be made over a sufficient distance

### Design Vehicle
- How vehicle characteristics affect highway design:
	- Vehicle width $\to$ lane width
	- Vehicle length $\to$ roadway capacity, turning radius
	- Vehicle height $\to$ the clearances of structures
	- Vehicle weight $\to$ structural design
	- Vehicle acceleration/deceleration $\to$ highway alignment
- AASHTO recommends four design vehicle classes:
1. Passenger cars
2. Buses
3. Trucks
4. Recreational Vehicles
- Select the largest vehicle that’s expected to use the roadway in significant numbers on a daily basis during the design year.
- Limit the speed differential between passenger cars and trucks to a max. of 10 mph. (upgrade, length of the upgrade

- Desirable spacing of access points or interchanges
- 1 mile in urban areas

### Alignment Design
- Highway centerline is a continuous and smooth curve in the 3D space
-  Horizontal alignment to determine coordinates (x, y) in horizontal plane
- Vertical alignment to determine coordinate (z) in elevation


![[Pasted image 20260910205159.png]]

- The contradiction between natural topography and design criteria
- An optimal alignment should best balance grade and curvature and satisfy design criteria
- Topography and alignment 
	- Level country: limited by land use, waterways, etc.
	- Rolling country: careful consideration of grade and curvature
	- Mountainous country: curvature is controlled by maximum grade criteria

### Horizontal Alignment
- Straight sections connected with circular curves to form smooth horizontal alignments
- Smooth:
	- left derivative = right derivative at the joint point
	- Tangents should match at the joint point
- A horizontal circular curve is a circular arc between two straight lines as tangents
- Back tangent = Approach tangent
- Forward tangent = Departure tangent

### Definition of Circular Curves 1
- In the U.S., curvature of city streets, highways, and railway can be specified by either the radius, R, or the degree of curve, D. There is no parallel concept in metric highway design.
- In most highway work, the length of the curve is understood to be the actual curved arc length, and the degree of the curve is the angle subtended by an arc of 100 ft.
-  When the degree of curve is related to an arc of 100 ft, it is said to be calculated on an arc basis.
