Given a two-way four-lane road with a circular curve with
2000’ radius to the right. The design speed is 60 mph. The
horizontal curve length is 820’. PC station is 14 + 10.00. The
superelevation required is 7%. The normal pavement crown is
2%. Calculate the stations where:
1) The left section starts to be raised
2) The left section’s grade becomes 0%
3) The right section starts to be lowered
4) The full superelevation is achieved
5) The full superelevation starts to be lowered
6) The right section changes back to a normal crown from full superelevation
7) The left section’s grade changes back to 0% from full superelevation
8) The left section changes back to a normal crown from full superelevation

First we use this table, which we use the US customary at 60 mph. we have a superelevation of 7, between 6 and 8. So we'll have to average between the two.
![[Pasted image 20260917205542.png]]

Note that the distance between A and B is **I**. To find **I**, it is also called the tangent runout length which is a simple equation as seen. Above. the Normal Cross-slope is 2%, while the superelevation rate is 7%. We multiply it by the minimum runoff of 280' to get an **I** of 80'

![[Pasted image 20260917205803.png]]

1. The first station (A) is when the left section starts to begin raising. To get there, we have to take the station PC (when the curve starts), and subtract it by $\frac{2L_{SE}}{3}$ and **I** 
2. The point where the left section is 0% grade (at station B) is just PC subtracted by $\frac{2L_{SE}}{3}$ 
   OR alternatively, add I from station A
3. Station C is when the right section starts to lower, **I** is the distance between AB and BC, so we can just add the distance of station B with **I** 
4. The point where superelevation is reached is at station D. We can add PC with $\frac{L_{SE}}{3}$. OR we can also add station B with $L_{SE}$ 
5. The full superelevation starts to be lowered (starts turning back to normal) is at station E. We subtract station PT (when the curve ends) by $\frac{L_{SE}}{3}$. 
6. The station where the right section returns to normal is at station F. To get to station F, we can use station G and subtract by **I**. 
7. To get station G in the first place, we can add PT with $\frac{2L_{SE}}{3}$. 
8. To get the point where the left section returns back to normal, it will be at station H. We add station G with **I** to get station H.
