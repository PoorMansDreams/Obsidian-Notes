### The importance of transportation systems
- provide mobility services for both people and goods, which are important for economic development and human needs.
	- 6% of the Gross National Products (GNP)
	- 11% of the labor force
	- 13% of all federal taxes

### Highway System Background Information 
- 50% of tonnage (tons of freights) is carried by trucks 
- about 2.75 trillion worth of miles have been services to passengers

- **Federal Road Act in 1916**
	- Starting modern era of highway federal aid
	- $75 million over five years to improve rural roads
	- The funds beign apportioned to each states based on state area, population and mileage of rural roads
	- States matching the federal funds on a 50-50 basis 

-  **Federal-Aid Highway Act in 1921**
	- Requiring each state to designate a connected system of interstate and intrastate routes
	- Placing responsibility for maintenance of these routes on each state

- **Federal-Aid Highway Act in 1944**
	- 500 million dollars are budgeted for each three years
	- Designation of two new highway systems:
		- National System of Interstate Highways
		- Principal Secondary Routes
	- ACT in 1952 and 1954 increased funds for National System of Interstate Highways

-  **Federal-Aid Highway Act in 1956**
	- A milestone in US highway development
	- $25 billion being authorizing to complete 41,000-mile national interstate highway system within 14 years
	- The largest peacetime public works program in world history
	- Federal funds being made available on 90-10 matching basis ($25 + $2.5 billion)

- **Highway Legislation of 1960’s recognizing growing transportation need of urban areas**
	- Act in 1962 requiring transportation planning process for federal-aid projects in urban areas with 50,000+ people (MPO: Metropolitan Planning Organization)
	- Act in 1968 authorized funds for parking facility in urban fringe area and established traffic operation program


• **Highway Legislation of 1970’s** 
- reflecting the desire to cope with increasing urbanization, traffic congestion, environmental degradation
	- 1970 Act authorized federal funds to be used for exclusive/preferential bus lanes, traffic control and parking facilities for public transit
	- 970 Act requires to consider adverse impact of federal project on many aspects including air, noise and water pollution, etc.

- **Highway Legislation of 1980’s**
	- Surface Transportation Assistance Act (STAA) of 1982
	- Addressed deteriorating highway infrastructure problems
		- Specified interstate 4-R program: resurfacing, restoration, rehabilitation and reconstruction
- **1987 Act**
	- $70 billion for federal highway program
	- $795 million for highway safety program

-  **Highway Legislation of 1990’s**
	- Intermodal Surface Transportation Efficiency Act (ISTEA) in 1991
		- $151 billion for both highway and transit
		- Dropped old classification: primary, secondary, urban and provided a new 155,000-mile national highway system
		- State and local governments take more responsibility for transportation policy
		- Requiring statewide transportation planning process and 2% of federal funds to be devoted to highway planning and research
- **1998 Transportation Equity Act for the 21 century (TEA-21)**
	- Strengthen transportation safety emphasis program
	- Investing in research like ITS (Intelligent Transportation System)

- **Highway Legislation of 2000’s**
	- Safe, Accountable, Flexible, Efficient Transportation Equity Act: A Legacy for Users (SAFETEA-LU) in 2005
		- $244 billion for both highway and transit
		- Safety: Doubling the funds for infrastructure to reduce highway fatalities
		- Innovative finance: attracting private sectors to participate in highway infrastructure projects (e.g. private activity bonds, tolling for infrastructure improvement)
		- Congestion relief: state can use road pricing to manage congestion and promote real-time traffic management
		- Environmental stewardship (non-motorized transportation)

- **Highway Legislation of 2010’s**
	- Moving Ahead for Progress in the 21st Century (MAP-21) Act, 2012
		- Authorized the federal surface transportation programs for highways, highway safety, and transit
		- Provided over $105 billion for fiscal years (FY) 2013 and 2014 (with extensions through 2015)
	- Fixing America’s Surface Transportation Act (FAST), 2015
		- Authorized the federal surface transportation program for highways, highway safety, transit, and rail
		- Provided $305 billion over 5 years

#### Research California SB1

Senate Bill 1, the [Road Repair and Accountability Act of 2017](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=201720180SB1), was signed into law on April 28, 2017. This legislative package invests $5.4 billion annually to fix roads, freeways and bridges in communities across California and puts more dollars toward transit and safety. These funds will be split equally between state and local investments.

- **Generates Revenue:** 
	- Raises 5-5.4 billion annually through **higher taxes** for fuel and vehicle registration
- **Splits Investments:** 
	- Divides funds evenly between state highway maintenance and local city and county road repairs.
- **Funds Public Transit:** 
	- Directs money toward local mass transit, intercity rail, and pedestrian or bicycle safety projects.
- **Protects Funds:** 
	- Ensures money raised goes strictly to transportation purposes rather than the state's general fund. 
#### Research Federal IIJA.

The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA), also known as the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, authorizes $1.2 trillion for transportation and infrastructure spending, including significant investments in roads, bridges, and broadband access. This act aims to improve public safety and create jobs across the country.

- **Modernize:**
	- Modernize the nation's infrastructure and create jobs while addressing issues of equity and environmental justic 
	- Targets Roads and Bridges for bridge repairs and major road projects
	- More money for Public transit and rail
- **Water**
	- Transport drinking water
	- Wastewater systems
	- replace lead pipes 
- **Power**
	- Upgrade grid reliability, improve resiliency
- **Environmental Resilience**
	- Improve infrastructure's resistance towards the weather, climate, and decrease pollution. 
- **More Pay**
	- Mandates that projects receiving IIJA funds pay workers wage rates consistent with the Davis-Bacon Act. 


### Highway Numbers

![[Pasted image 20260903211340.png]]
**Even Numbers** go east-west, 
**Odd Numbers** go north-south 

For the **East-West signs,** the south side has low numbers, and the north side has high numbers
For the **North-South signs**, the west side has low numbers, and the east side has high numbers. 
![[Pasted image 20260903211645.png|700]]

Three-Digit signs are extra highways that branch off the two-digit interstates

If the first digit is **even**, the highway starts and ends at another two-digit highway 

If the first digit is odd, the highway ends at a non-two digit highway, (could just end or end in a city or something)
