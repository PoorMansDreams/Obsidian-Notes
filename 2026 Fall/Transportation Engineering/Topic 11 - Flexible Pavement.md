- Flexible Pavement 
	- Made of asphalt; but only the surface.
	- Thickness: ranges from 1" to 6" depending on light/heavy traffic
		- Purpose of a surface course:
			- withstand wear and tear of moving vehicles
			- sufficient stability to prevent [^1]shoving and [^2]rutting
			- prevent the entrance of excessive surface water into base/subgrade
- Layers in flexible pavement
	- Base Grade
		- High stability and density, not damaged by capillary water/frost action
		- distribute or spread thee stress from surface course
		- prevent subgrade from excessive deformation
	- Subbase Course (optional)
		- Consisting of granular materials
		- Used when subgrade is extremely weak, frost action is severe
		- Suitable subbase materials are chepaer than base material of high quality
	- Subgrade
		- Also called foundation layer; supports all loads that come onto the pavement
		- made of natural earth of compacted soil
- Design Principle
	- The combined thickness of subbase base and surface course must be strong enough to handle traffic loads for design life
		- Influential Factors: 
			- Traffic loading
				- Magnitude of axle load
				- volume and composition of axle load
				- Tire Pressure and contact area 
					- Total estimated magnitude and occurence of traffic loadings: **Equivalent Single Axle Load (ESAL)** usually 18 kips for single axle loads
			- Climate or environment
				- Temperature and Moisture
				- High temp causes asphalt concrete to lose strability
				- Low temp causes asphalt concrete to be hard and stiff
				- Temp and mist fluctuation have frost-heave/frost-thaw damage
			- Material characteristics
				- Asphalt surface: strength or stability
				- Granular base/subbase: gradation, strength, or stability
				- Subgrade: strength or stability, soil classification, repeated load properties
				- Usually tested using CBR test

#### California Bearing Ratio (CBR) Test
Used to determine the required unit load for different penetrations, typically 0.1"
the loads are compared with a value of 1000$\frac{\text{lb}}{\text{in}^2}$ 
$$
\text{CBR\%}=\frac{\text{unit load at }0.1\text{ inch penetration}}{1000}\times 100
$$
- Some CBR ranges
	- California limestone (100)
	- High quality crushed rock (80)
	- Farmland soil (3)
