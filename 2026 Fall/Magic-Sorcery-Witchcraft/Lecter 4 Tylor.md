Sir Edward Burnett Tylor (1832-1917)
Author of *Primitive Culture*, was into Darwinian. Never held a university degree. Was primarily a armchair anthropologist. 

Founder of cultural anthropology. "Father of anthropology". Culture/Civilization is a broad complex whole that includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, customs, and every habit acquired by man as a member of society. 

Tylor believed that Magic went in stages, 
Animism $\rightarrow$ Totemism $\rightarrow$ Polytheism $\rightarrow$  Monotheism

All people are equally intelligent, but education brings about civilization-without it, "primitive" peoples are like children. Argues that "set of savages" is like any other: same spears, myths, textiles, baskets, etc. regardless of place. 

Tylor believed that all religious ideas had developed out of primitive belief in the animate nature of natural phenomena. In short, Tylor believed that religion is a mistaken attempt to make sense of the physical world in which we live, as rational as science but simply erroneous. 

Tylor tells us that the races, in complexity from least to greatest was Australian, Tahitian, Aztec, Chinese, and Italian.

Correcting Tylor

Tylor believed that civilization is the general improvement of mankind by higher organization of the individual and of society, to the end of promoting man's goodness, power, and happiness. Primitive peoples ignore science and evidence, he claims, and prioritize the "knowledge" of their ancestors. 

Tylor's ideas considers "lower races" to be violent, native savagery, and cannibalism. Thinks that all people will develop and will not regress.
