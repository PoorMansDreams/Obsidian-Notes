Written by Heinrich Kramer, (aka Henry Institoris), a Catholic Dominican monk and inquisitor in 1486, Jacob Sprenger was a co-author but it's not clear how much he wrote. 

Kramer was expelled from Innsbruck for being kind of crazy and obsessed with interrogating women about their sex acts, Jacob and Kramer wrote this book after. 

Kramer interrogated Helena Scheuberin, who was known to be an independent and assertive woman, Kramer wanted to establish that she was a witch. Kramer wrote the book a year after interrogating Helena

Helena said "Fie on you, you bad monk, may the falling evil take you." Basically saying that he was a terrible man (he was). 
When Helena was interrogated, they asked her about her prior virginity and sexual history, making everyone kind of uncomfortable, and then he was run out of town for it.

Kramer retreats to Colgne, where he wrote the Malleus Maleficarum (Witch's Hammer), Before he wrote this book, Europeans believed in maleficia, evil female spirits, but the idea of Witches haven't been established fully. 

The Catholic Inquisition and the Pope in 1484, supported Kramer with writing his book about witches. He argued that witches were real, it could be any woman, and they were a great danger to society. 
	The book justified mass sexual assault and rape against women, (and some men), giving their tortures impunity (people who tortured witches were not punished)

Before the witch craze in Europe, people spoke of evil acts, sorcerers, heretics, but didn't target witches specifically. The Malleus Maleficarum influenced society greatly. Witches were imagined as an agent with the devil's powers
	Malleus identified a wide range of acts as something a witch would do. (even somewhat normal things)

* Kramer argued that all women were capable of being seduced into witchcraft
	* All witchcraft came from carnal lust, which is in women insatiable
	* he feared of magically induced impotence, penis being stolen/hidden 
	* Even if magic merely created the illusion of penis disappearance, the impotence created by witches was as traumatic as a castration.
