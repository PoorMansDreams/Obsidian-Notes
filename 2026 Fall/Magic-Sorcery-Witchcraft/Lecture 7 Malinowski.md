#Magic
Bronislaw Malinowski 1884-1942 Actually traveled to places he wrote about, was more sympathetic to magic and religious.

Malinowski believes that magic is a form of pseudo science. People carrying religious acts or magical acts are not crazy, they are doing it to do something positive for their community. 

Malinowski participates with the acts and things that people do. 

- Malinowski distinguishes magic from science, but argues that magic, science, and religion compliment each other
- Unlike Taylor and Frazer, he does not believe in an evolution of these concepts - they CO-EXIST.
- Believed that magic involves acts that are a means to a desired end, while religion functions to stabilize a community (agrees with Durkheim)

Thinks that magic may help psychologically for the practitioner. Malinowski was a functionalist

People he studied did not prioritize magic over science, but both were important. As he studied, he noticed that these people don't actually 100% believe in whatever magic they are doing will work. 

---

Malinowski points out that humans have a strong drive to overcome their challenge. This strong drive can lead to them carrying magical acts like imitating killing an enemy - and that very behavior helps us feels better. (sort of like projecting or manifesting)

Believed that magic served a purpose. Carrying a magical ritual helps you psychologically. Without magic to make us optimistic, we would not have triumphed as a species. 

Religion was more communal than magic, (They believed). Believed that the function of magic is to ritualize man's optimism, to enhance his faith in the victory of hope over fear. 

Basically wanted to say that **Magic helps people**.

Believed that religious/magical acts helped soothes people's anxieties, and THATS why they do them.

Argued against Tylor and Frazer, that all people are aware of and practice both science and religion/magic.
