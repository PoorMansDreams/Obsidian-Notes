Engineering Education
- In the US you **need** a license to practice professional civil engineering. 
- Licensure requirements vary slightly by state
- Usually, an educational requirement must be met.

- Licensure is a collaboration between
	- Education - ABET (Accreditation)
	- Government - NCEES, Licensing Board
	- Industry - Professional Organization

- Major Changes in engineering education
- 1955 - Engineering was mode to be more math/science based
- 1965 - Goal Report: more official recognition of the role of graduate study, especially the Master's degree program 
- 1980-1990 - Engineering Education were examined, over 20 major studies took place.

### Study Findings 
Some common criticisms of engineering graduates by various studies:
-  Graduates lack the ability to step into a job and become immediately productive.
- Education for management is lacking.
- Writing and communication skills are not adequately emphasized.
- Curriculum must be expanded to include non-technical subjects.
- Exposure to cultural and regional differences to become competitive in foreign markets.

- In 2001, an ASCE report “Engineering the future of Civil Engineering” highlighted the complexity, the challenges, and the rapid changes confronting the profession.
- The report concluded that the current four-year bachelor’s degree is becoming inadequate formal academic preparation for the practice of civil engineering at the professional level in the 21st century.
- This lead to ASCE’s Raising the Bar and Engineer Tomorrow initiatives

### What Does an Engineer Need to Know?
- TCAP - Master plan for the Body of Knowledge was the foundation
- A BOK Committee was created May 2002 to define the BOK needed to enter the practice of civil engineering at the professional level
- The BOK Committee defined the BOK through a series of outcomes; knowledge, skills, attitudes necessary to become a licensed professional engineer
	- How to Obtain a BOK
		- Undergraduate Education (UG) leading to a BSCE from a four-year program accredited by ABET.
		- Postgraduate Education (PG) equivalent or leading to a MSCE, equivalent to one year full-time study
		- Mentored Experience (ME) in one’s early career under the guidance of a practicing civil engineer progressing in both complexity and level of responsibility.
		- Self-Developed (SD) through formal or informal activities and personal observation and reflection.
### Accreditation
- Types of Accreditation
	- There are two types of accreditation: institutional and specialized.
	- Institutional accreditation evaluates overall institutional quality. One form of institutional accreditation is regional accreditation of colleges and universities.
	- Specialized accreditation examines specific programs of study, rather than an institution as a whole. This type of accreditation is granted to specific programs at specific levels. Architecture, nursing, law, medicine, and engineering programs are often evaluated through specialized accreditation
### ABET
- ABET
	- ABET, Inc., is the recognized U.S. accreditor of college and university programs in applied science, computing, engineering, and technology. Accreditation ensures the quality of the postsecondary education students receive.
	- ABET was established in 1932 and is now a federation of 35 professional and technical societies representing the fields of applied science, computing, engineering, and technology.
	- ABET currently accredits some 4,307 programs at over 846 colleges and universities in 41 countries.
	- Mutual Recognition Agreements & Accreditation

### Graduate Education
- Graduate study has become increasingly more important
- In 1997 the number of engineering receiving master's degrees was almost half the number receiving bachelor's degrees. 
- Graduate study may lead to a Master of Science (M.S.) in Engineering and to a Ph.D
