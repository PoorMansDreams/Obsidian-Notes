
### Location Selection
- Selecting the location of a proposed highway is an important initial step is design.
- Factors influencing the location selection include:
	- Topography
	- Soil Characteristics
	- Environmental Factors
	- Cost
- Required data are obtained from different types of surveys

### Principals of Highway Location

Roadway elements such as curvature and grade much blend with each other to produce a system that provides for the easy and safe flow.

The Highway should cause a minimal disruption to historic and archeological siters and to other land-use activities. 

### Phases of Highway Location Process
- Office study or existing information
	- Examination of all available data pertaining to the area
	- These data can be obtained from existing engineering reports, maps, aerial photographs. 
	- Examples of Evidence that can be found
		- Engineering  - topography, geology, climate, traffic
		- Social - land use, zoning, population patterns
		- Environmental - wildlife heritage air/noise/water
		- Economic - construction costs and activity trends 
- Reconnaissance survey
	- To identify several feasible routes
	- Terrain and soil conditions
	- Serviceability of route to industrial and population areas
	- Crossing of other transportation facilities, such as water bodies railroads and highways
	- Directness of route 
- Preliminary location survey
	- The positions of the feasible routes are set as closely as possible by establishing al control points and determining preliminary vertical and horizonal alignments for each
	- Preliminary alignments are used to evaluate the economic and environmental feasibility of the alternative routes 
- Final Location survey

### Economic Evaluation
- To determine the future effects of investigating the resources necessary to construct the highway.
- Factors include
	- Road user costs
	- Construction costs
	- Maintenance costs
	- road user benefits and
	- any disbenefits, which may include adverse impacts due to dislocation of families and businesses.
