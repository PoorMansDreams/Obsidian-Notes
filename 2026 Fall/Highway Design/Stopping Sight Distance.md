Stopping Sight Distance is the distance it takes for the driver to come to a complete stop after first seeing or reacting to the object on the road.

### Problem 1
---
Calculate the length of a vertical curve to provide SSD of 710 ft at a crest, with 3.4% ascending grade and 4% descending grade? (to the nearest)

Given that the SSD is 710', $g_{1}=3.4\%$ and $g_{2}=-4\%$. We can calculate the A
$A=|g_{2}-g_{1}|=7.4\%$. We can now assume either $S<L$ or $S>L$. 

The equation we are using assumes that $S<L$

$$
L=\frac{As^2}{2158}=\frac{7.4\times 710^2}{2158}=1728.6\text{ ft}
$$

The assumption that $L>S$ is correct. We are good.

### Problem 2
---
A vertical curve is being designed to connect two grades: $g_{1}=2.8\%$ and $g_{2}=-2.3\%$. If the **design speed on this road is 55 mph** and the posted speed limit is 45 mph, the minimum length of vertical curve to satisfy AASHTO safe stopping sight distance criteria is most nearly? 

Looking at the table 3-35, The SSD for a 55 mph design speed is **495 ft**. 

Given this, we need to assumed again, either $S<L$ or $S>L$. 

$$
A=|-2.3-2.8|=5.1\%
$$

Assume that $L>S$

$$
L=\frac{As^2}{2158}=\frac{5.1\times 495^2}{2158}=579.06\text{ ft}
$$
Our assumption is correct.
