### What is geometric design?

- Geometric design determines the physical layout and imensions of the transportation facilities
	- The goal is to develop a design that is:
		- Safe
		- comfortable
		- efficient
		- economical 
		- Constructible
		- Compatible with the surrounding environment (it doesn't mess up the ground)

### Why Does Geometric Design Matter?
- Safety
	- Sight distance (cant see)
	- Appropriate Geometry for the Design speed (sharp turns are bad)
- Minimizing Costs
	- Earth Work (cut/fill)
	- Land acquisition and construction easements 
- Environment
	- Plant (Don't want to cut through trees and nature)
	- Wildlife Habitats (Leave the animals alone)
	- Water Body (Water of the US/Natural Streams) (fish)

### Horizontal Alignment
- A horizontal alignment defines the path of highway in plan view
- In highway design the horizontal alignment typically represents:
	- Highway centerline
		- The Centerline is the middle of the road, it could also be between two roads on a highway
	- Ramp baseline 

### Stationing
- Stationing is a measurement that used to identify locations along an alignment
- One station equals 100 feet
- STA 1+00 = 100ft
- This helps engineers and contractors determine:
	- Length of roadway segments
	- Beginning/end of curve
	- Change in geometry
	- Cross section transitions.

### Why Do We Need Horizontal Curves?
- Roadways need to change directions due to existing constraints 
	- Terrain
		- Moving, Filling, Cutting existing land is expensive
	- Existing Development
		- We may have to go around homes, buying or acquiring that land is expensive 
	- Cost
	- Environment 
		- There may be endangered species or protected wildlife in the area we want to go through
- We cannot do straight lines when making roads because of these constraints 
- A Horizontal curve provides a gradual change in direction between two roadway tangents (we try to avoid a bunch of stuff, meaning we'll need to make the road curvy)

### What Controls Horizontal Curves
- Design Speed
- When driving through a curve, you may feel like you are being pushed towards the outside of the curve the vehicle requires a centripetal force towards the center of the curve to maintain its curved path 
- From the centripetal formula, $F=\frac{MV^2}{R}$ 


- The Design process of the Horizontal Curve is to first draw straight lines, and then connect them with tangent curves
