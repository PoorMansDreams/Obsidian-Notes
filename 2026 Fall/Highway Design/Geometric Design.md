### Design Hourly Volume
	- Projected hourly volume that is used for design
	- The 1st hour is the highest volume of vehicles in an hour, the 30th hour is essentially how much traffic you expect, and so, what you design for. 
- 30th highest hourly volume is usually selected as the DHV (Design hourly volume)
- $DHV=K\times ADT$
	- 30th highest hourly volume is typically 12-18% of ADT with avg approximately 15% 
	- ADT = Average Daily Traffic
	- K = % of daily traffic that happens during peak hours

- Rolling is a up and down but stays consistent, Mountainous is mainly uphill.
- The speed goes higher when there's more cars because then we design for a highway, for less than 400, a highway or freeway isn't needed, so we make a local road (hence why its slower) 
-  Selected speed to determine the various geometric features of the roadway.
- Depends on the functional classification of the highway, topography, traffic volume and land use of adjacent areas.
- For highway design, topography is generally classified into three groups: level, rolling and mountainous.

### Clear Zone

![[Pasted image 20260923174121.png]]

- Backslope = Cut area
- Foreslope = fill area 
	- Any slope 4H:1V is recoverable (vehicles can climb back up). Anything steeper and vehicles cannot climb back up (unrecoverable)

- The total roadside border area, available for use by errant vehicles, starting at the edge of traveled way.
- This area may consist of a shoulder, a recovery slope, a non-recoverable slope, and/or a clear run-out area.
- The design clear zone guidance is a function of the Design speed, side slopes and traffic volumes.
- Slope 4H:1V is recoverable and anything steeper than that is considered non-recoverable.

![[Pasted image 20260923174333.png]]![[Pasted image 20260923174416.png]]

Note - if a project does not say there is a required foreslope, take the maximum. 
- Length of the None-recoverable slope should be the same length as the clear runout area, But with a minimum of 10 ft. 
	- When the design speed increases, the recovery zone will become longer. 

![[Pasted image 20260923175048.png]]

- Horizontal Curve Adjustment Factors will change the size of the clear zone as well. 
	- In general, a higher speed and smaller radius will increase the Horizontal Curve Adjustment Factor.

### Example Problems 

![[Pasted image 20260923175402.png]]

$$
\text{Clear Zone}=CZ_{0}=L_{0}\times K_{CZ}
$$
First we need to find the clear zone distance, and then multiply it by the horizontal curve adjustment factor. When taking the clear zone range, it was 24-30ft. But since "minimum" wasn't mentioned, we take the max, 30 ft. 

$$
\begin{align}
L_{0}=30\text{ ft} \\
K_{CZ}=1.4 \\
\text{Clear Zone}=CZ_{0}=30\text{ ft}\times 1.4 = 42\text{ ft}
\end{align}
$$
Remember, the clear zone is the **shoulder** and the **recoverable/nonrecoverable/runout slopes.** 
To find X, we will need to subtract 8 ft from 42 ft. 
$$
\begin{align}
X&=CZ_{0}-\text{Shoulder Width} \\
X&=42\text{ ft }-8\text{ ft}=\boxed{34\text{ ft}}
\end{align}
$$
