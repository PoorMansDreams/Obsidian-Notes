### Design Hourly Volume
	- Projected hourly volume that is used for design
	- The 1st hour is the highest volume of vehicles in an hour, the 30th hour is essentially how much traffic you expect, and so, what you design for. 
- 30th highest hourly volume is usually selected as the DHV (Design hourly volume)
- $DHV=K\times ADT$
	- 30th highest hourly volume is typically 12-18% of ADT with avg approximately 15% 
	- ADT = Average Daily Traffic
	- K = % of daily traffic that happens during peak hours

-  Selected speed to determine the various geometric features of the roadway.

- For highway design, topography is generally classified into three groups: level, rolling and mountainous.

### Clear Zone

![[Pasted image 20260923174121.png]]

- Backslope = Cut area
- Foreslope = fill area 
	- Any slope 4H:1V is recoverable (vehicles can climb back up). Anything steeper and vehicles cannot climb back up (unrecoverable)

- The total roadside border area, available for use by errant vehicles, starting at the edge of traveled way.
- This area may consist of a shoulder, a recovery slope, a non-recoverable slope, and/or a clear run-out area.
- The design clear zone guidance is a function of the Design speed, side slopes and traffic volumes.
- Slope 4H:1V is recoverable and anything steeper than that is considered non-recoverable.

	- When the design speed increases, the recovery zone will become longer.
