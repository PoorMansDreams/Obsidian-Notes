1. What happens to our waste water? Or in another word, where did last night's dinner go?
It goes straight to the sewer system, it is sent to a water treatment facility, then dispersed back into the environment in different forms, Agriculture for example.

2. What happens if we release untreated wastewater to water bodies (streams, river ocean, etc)
Eventually, the wastes in the wastewater get spread into the environment, causing oxygen levels in the water to decrease. The microorganisms live off the dissolved oxygen. Less oxygen means less microorganisms, and this effects the food chain. Eventually leading to less life overall. 

3. Can you explain some of the wastewater treatment steps? '
Coagulation - some small floating particles that cannot be removed by settling or filtration, then we use a coagulant (chemical) that clump and stick to small particles, making them bigger, and easier to filter/settle. Injecting Oxygen into wastewater for bacteria to break down the stuff that was in the water. We then disinfect using chlorine.

Wastewater treatment steps
- NY 1.3 billion gallons of sewage, 14 different plants

 (Pretreatment)
- First step: Bar screens: simply a giant filter to stop large physical objects from getting into the system
- Grit Chamber: the viscous sewage sometimes have sands or solid particles that need to be filtered, use a vortex, horizontal, or wall grit chamber.
- Clarifier: Wastewater is let to sit and flow around, but flows slow enough for more particles to settle at the bottom.
- Coagulation Flocculation inorganics removal.
(Effluent) (smaller than 10 micrometers)
- Most of the stuff in the water now is organic
- Aeration Basins - blows oxygen into the wastewater, allows aerobic digestion (Breakdown of organic matter with the use of excess oxygen)
- The wastewater is then moved to a second clarifier, where more bio-organic stuff settles 
- 85% of organic stuff is removed from the water at this point
Disinfection
- Chlorine, Oxone, UV. 
- Chlorine: chemical added to kill all bacteria
- Ozone: putting electricity into the water causes ozone to form, which causes the bacteria's cell wall to leak and die
- Ultraviolet Treatment: the bacteria is unable to reproduce after treatment and don't do anything

effluent discharge: 
full cycle water reuse, the entire process takes about 23-36 hours to fully do

EPA gives necessary adjustments to ensure proper discharge and treatment.
