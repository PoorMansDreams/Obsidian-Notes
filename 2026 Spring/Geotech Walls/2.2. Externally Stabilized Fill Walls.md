There are different ways a wall can fail as well,
- Sliding
	- The whole wall slides horizontally
- Overturning
	- The whole wall tips over on on the bottom corner
- Overstressing
	- the wall rotates on the toe, one corner goes down and the opposite end corner goes up
- Global Failure 
	- The wall rotates around the stem or somewhere along the top of the wall.

We will have to calculate the forces on the wall. The forces are are follows 

Weight of the Wall in $\text{lb/ft}$
$$
W_{1}=B\cdot L \cdot \gamma_{\text{concrete}}
$$
If the wall has a slope, divide it into a triangle and a rectangle
$$
W_{2}= \frac{1}{2}B\cdot H \cdot \gamma_{\text{concrete}}
$$
Now we have to calculate the force of the soil onto the wall 
$$
E_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(H-Z_{cr})\cdot \sigma'_{h}
$$
note, $Z_{cr}$ is "Z-crack" which is $Z_{cr}=\frac{2c}{\gamma}$

We will then have to check for the sliding and tipping, which means we will have to calculate the distances the forces are from the toe of the wall, and the center of the wall (at the bottom)

multiply the distances with the forces and sum together. 

To check for sliding...

$$
F.S.=\frac{\Sigma\text{ F Resisting}}{\Sigma\text{ F sliding}}=\frac{\text{Friction}}{\text{Soil Pushing Against Wall}}=\frac{W\times \tan \delta+(B\cdot c')}{E_{a}}
$$
remember, $\delta=\phi \times\text{ratio}$  

To check for overturning/tipping...

$$
F.S.=\frac{\Sigma\text{ Moment Stabilizing}}{\Sigma \text{ Moment Turning}}=\frac{\Sigma W_{i}x_{i}}{E_{a}y_{E}}
$$
Note, the stabilizing moments will rotate "against" the soil, while the moment turning will rotate "away" from the soil
