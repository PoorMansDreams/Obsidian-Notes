Soldier pile walls are made of vertical H-beams. With wood or concrete "laggings" (planks), that are put in-between the H-beams. As we excavate, the laggings will drop down, and we simply add more at the top to fall down. 

The giant metal things sticking out of the walls are called compression props.

![[Pasted image 20260414143210.png]]

These excavation machines are made to cut very deep into the ground (uses drilling mud). The drilling mud (bentonite+water) has high density, and actually supports the soil. If we let the drilling mud set after drilling the hole. It will turn into a slurry wall. But we don't want that. 

We drill a hole with drilling mud, then the hole will be full of drilling mud.
What we do is we use a tremie (long funnel) to pour concrete down into the hole, WHILE the drilling mud is still in the hole. The concrete is heavier than the drilling mud, so the mud will float to the top, while the concrete fills the bottom. Eventually the concrete will replace the drilling mud. Let it set, and it'll become a strong reinforced concrete wall. (There is some rebar hung in the concrete while it's being poured)

Anchored struts don't protrude into the excavated area, so they don't get in the way. However, the anchor may enter a zone we cannot touch, (issues with right of way). We also will need to excavate the side we aren't working on. 

The way these work, is that the plate experiences passive earth pressure (soil pushes on the plate to the right). The active earth pressure (pushing to the left) will push on the wall. There is a tension cable that connects the plate and the wall. In short, we are using the passive earth pressure to fight against the active earth pressure

![[Pasted image 20260414150631.png]]

These work with most types of walls. As we start excavating on one side of the wall, we can dig a angled hole, fill it with grout connected to a tension cable. Continue excavating, then keep repeating until we reach our desired depth. It doesn't need to be in the passive area like the deadman do. These just need to be outside of the active wedge area. 

![[Pasted image 20260421141529.png]]

The dredge line is also known as the excavation line, which is the level where we stop excavating, or removing soil. When soil is removed from one side of the wall, it will cause the soil to relax, and apply pressure to the wall. 

due to the force acting on the top half of the wall, it causes a moment force to happen at the wall still below the soil. If we have a prop (support, like a beam of some sort). The reactional force we want is in the opposite direction of the lateral earth pressure. 

We want to push against the lateral earth pressure essentially. We can optimize the design, we can shorten the depth of the wall in the soil if we have a prop (support) on the wall. We want to do this because usually the embedment is quite deep. The depth of the cantilever wall is usually 2 to 1.5 times the height of the soil. It can get expensive and difficult to install these walls.

We will start with a cantilever wall. 

Find $K_{a}$ and $K_{p}$ 
$$
K_{a}=\frac{1-\sin 30}{1+\sin30}=\frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
K_{p}\times K_{a}=1
$$
$$
K_{p}=3
$$
Start by finding lateral earth pressure for 3 main points
units are in $\frac{\text{lb}}{\text{ft}^2}$
$$
\begin{align}
e_{1}=0 \\
e_{2}=(103\times 15')\times \frac{1}{3}=515 \\
e_{3}=-[515+103(u+x)(K_{a}-K_{p})]
\end{align}
$$

we are unable to calculate what u or x is, so we can't find what $e_{3}$ is, but we can instead find a point where the lateral earth pressure is 0, at $e_4$ the lateral earth pressure is net 0. (it is where the left and right side lateral earth pressure forces cancel eachother out)

$$
\begin{align}
e_{4}=515+\gamma u(K_{a}-K_{p}) \\
0=515+103\times u\left( \frac{1}{3} -3\right) \\
u=1.875'
\end{align}
$$

We can further simplify our $e_{3}$ equation knowing that the forces at $e_{4}$ is net zero.

$$
e_{3}=\gamma(K_{p}-K_{a})\times x
$$
$$
e_{3}=274.67x
$$

Now, we find all the forces from the 3 triangles, 

$$
\begin{align}
E_{1}=\frac{1}{2}e_{2}H=3862.5 \\
E_{2}=\frac{1}{2}e_{2}u=482.8125 \\
E_{e}=\frac{1}{2}e_{3}x=137.335x^2
\end{align}
$$

We can now calculate what the sum moment is, since this is a static structure we will set $\sum M=0$
The moment will be at around point "o" which is the bottom of the wall.
$$
0=E_{1}\left( \frac{15}{3} +u+x\right)+E_{2}\left( \frac{2}{3}u+x \right)+E_{3}\left( \frac{1}{3}x \right)
$$

Plug in our values, and we will see that $x=12'$
