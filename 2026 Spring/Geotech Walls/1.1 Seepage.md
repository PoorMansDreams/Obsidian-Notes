Water seepage can be seen in this equation: $\sigma'=\sigma-u$ the $u$ is the seepage, it basically takes away the strength of the soil. It causes our factory of safety to falter. 

 * Permeability is the ability for a porous medium (e.g., soil) to allow the permeation of a fluid (e.g., water)
* It is the measure of how easily a fluid can pass through a porous medium
generally, soil is permeable, and water flows through it quite often. 

There can only be movement in the water if the TOTAL head is different, total head is simply Bernoulli's 
$$
h=\frac{p}{\gamma}+z+\frac{v^2}{2g}
$$
When it comes to soils, sands are very permeable, and water slowly flows through it.
**note** in soils, $v\approx0$, hence head by velocity is also 0.
The total head from fluids in a soil can be written as 
$$
h\approx z+\frac{p}{\gamma}
$$
as the water flows through the soil, there is a flow direction, and perpendicular to them are equipotential lines. As the water flows through the equipotential lines, it loses its head energy.

Example; water at 4 head flows through soil to reach 0 head. If the water flows 1/4th through the soil, it'll only have 3/4ths of the original head, in short, it'll only have 3 head after the water flows 1/4th through the soil.

$\boxed{n_{f}=\text{flow channels}}$
$\boxed{n_{d}=\text{number of equipotential drops}}$ 

since $\frac{n_{f}}{n_{d}}$ is a ratio, the number of flow channels and equipotential drops will still give you the same ratio or number. 

### Example Problem 1 
![[Pasted image 20260129140555.png]]

Lets say the wall is 12 feet deep inside the soil, and the soil is 25' deep. 

First, assume the depth of the water, in this case, H = 11'
we also say that we have 9 flow channels ($n_{f}$) (blocks in-between the blue lines), and 18 equipotential drops ($n_{d}$) (red lines) **Equipotential lines will always tell us TOTAL HEAD the water has at this particular location**

First, we calculate the $\Delta h$, 
$$
\begin{align}
H&=\Delta h\cdot n_{d} \\
\Delta h&=\frac{H}{n_{d}} =\frac{11}{18}=0.61
\end{align}
$$
We can also calculate the $Q_{total}$. The 1/30.48 is a conversion factor to convert cm/s
$$
Q_{total}=10^{-6}\cdot 11\cdot \frac{9}{18}\cdot\left( \frac{1}{30.48} \right)=1.8\times 10^{-6} \text{ cfs} 
$$
cfs = cubic feet per second

We are calculating for $u$ which is **pore water pressure**

At point A, we can easily calculate by plugging in knowns
$$
\begin{align}
h_{t_{1}A}&=h_{z_{1}A}+h_{p_{1}A} \\
H-3\Delta h&=-6+h_{p_{1}A} \\
h_{p_{1}A}&=6+11-3(0.61)=15.17 \text{ feet} \\
15.17\text{ feet}\times \gamma_{w} (\text{lb/ft}^3)&= 946.4 \frac{\text{lb}}{\text{ft}^2}=u_{A}
\end{align}

$$

Similarly for Point B, 
$$
\begin{align}
h_{t_{1}B}&=h_{z_{1}B}+h_{p_{1}B} \\
H-15\Delta h&=-6+h_{p_{1}B} \\
h_{p_{1}A}&=6+11-15(0.61)=7.83 \text{ feet} \\
7.85\text{ feet}\times \gamma_{w} (\text{lb/ft}^3)&= 488.5 \frac{\text{lb}}{\text{ft}^2}=u_{B}
\end{align}

$$
and for Point C, 
$$
\begin{align}
h_{t_{1}C}&=h_{z_{1}C}+h_{p_{1}C} \\
H-15\Delta h&=-12+h_{p_{1}C} \\
h_{p_{1}C}&=12+11-15(0.61)=13.83 \text{ feet} \\
13.83\text{ feet}\times \gamma_{w} (\text{lb/ft}^3)&= 862.99 \frac{\text{lb}}{\text{ft}^2}=u_{C}
\end{align}

$$
This is how we solve for pore water pressure in certain points under the soil.
