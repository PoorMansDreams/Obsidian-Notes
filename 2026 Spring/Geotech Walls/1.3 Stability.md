There are three types of main slope failures, 
* Translational Slope failure (infinite slope)
* Rotational Slope Failure
* Block Slope Failure

The equations that relate to this are 
$$
\begin{align}
FS=\frac{c'}{\gamma z\cos \beta \sin \beta}+\frac{\tan \phi'}{\tan \beta}
\end{align}
$$

Where $c'$ is cohesion, and $\gamma$ is the specific weight

if the cohesion is 0, then the equation entirely relies on the right side. (if the slope is steeper, FS will be less, if the slope is milder, the FS will be more)

The angle of repose is the angle at which a pile of soil can stand at before it "gives"
for example, sand goes down through a funnel, and the sand makes a small pile. As the sand piles up on the ground, it makes a conical pyramid. The horizontal angle the pyramid has stays constant, even as the sand piles up. The sand just slides down. This angle is the **angle of repose**, also known as $\phi$
![[1.3 Sand Example|200]]
for a **fully submerged slope** we must use a different $\gamma$ because of the water
$$
\begin{align}
FS=\frac{c'}{\gamma' z\cos \beta \sin \beta}+\frac{\tan \phi'}{\tan \beta}
\end{align}
$$

### Rotational slope analysis

In this case the failure moment is in an arc. We need to find the Factor of Safety for one failure surface. 
$$
FS=\frac{\sum\text{Resisting Moment}}{\sum\text{Driving Moment}}=\frac{\sum M_{R}}{\sum M_{D}}
$$
To put it simply, $M_{R}$ is how much the soil can resist while $M_{D}$ is the force acting on the soil. In this case, The only force acting on the soil is the **weight of the soil** itself. 
![[Pasted image 20260210141220.png]]

To simplify doing this, we can cut the slope into smaller easier shapes. A method to do this is to draw a line that connects one end of the arc to the other. the leftover distance that isn't sloped is the $x_{\text{crest}}$ we use this, with the Height to find the area of that triangle
$$
A_{1}=x_{\text{crest}}\times H \times \frac{1}{2}
$$
We then find the centroid of the triangle by drawing bisectors, the lines go from the middle of a side to the corner of the triangle. 

To find the centroid of the sector, we know it is located somewhere in the middle of the centroid. In a rectangular body, we know it is in the center. in a triangle, we know it is 1/3rd from the base. so knowing this, we know that the centroid is somewhere between the two other centroids.

$$
l_{i}=R\theta_{\text{radians}}
$$

![[Stability Circle]]
Area of a sector is 
$$
\frac{1}{2}R^2\theta_{\text{rad}}
$$
However, in our shape, we just want the "crust" of this sector slice. So we subtract the triangle in there. The area of the triangle is $\frac{1}{2}R^2\sin \theta$ so then
$$
A_{2}=\frac{1}{2}R^2\theta_{\text{rad}}-\frac{1}{2}R^2\sin \theta
$$
We had to find the area of the soils because that's the only way to get the **weight** (we assume the thickness of the slope is 1 unit)

NOTE THAT WE USE $c=\tau_{f}=Su$ AND $l=R\theta$


Drawing a line vertically down from the center of the arc, we get $x$. If we multiply this $x$ with $W$, the weight of the soil, this gives us the $M_{D}$ of the soil, or the force acting on the soil.
$$
\sum M_{D}=W_{1}x_{1}
$$

To calculate $M_{R}$ or the resistance of the ==sods==[^1], we get
$$
M_{R}=clR
$$
in the end, our equation for the factor of safety is
$$
FS=\frac{\sum c_{i}l_{i}R}{\sum W_{i}x_{i}}
$$

Another issue that might occur is if the soil is drained. This changes the equation for $\tau_{f}$. Note that $\sigma'$ is normal to the failure surface 
$$
\begin{align}
\tau_{f}=c'+\sigma'\tan \phi &\text{ drained} \\
\tau_{f}=S_{u} &\text{ undrained}
\end{align}
$$
through the sections of the slope, the $\phi$ is different, so we cannot use the Swedish Circle to solve. 
![[Pasted image 20260212140546.png]]

We cut the slopes vertically, the bottom portion of the trapeziums can be assumed to be straight. 
**area of a trapezium is**
$$
A=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{2})\times h
$$
Note that the height is measured by the middle of the bottom length to the top
![[Stability Geowalll Area ex|80]]

note that $l=\frac{B_{1}}{\cos \alpha}$

we can also assume that the **centroid** is in the center of the trapezium, it's very similar to a rectangle to it's fine.
We assume that $\sigma'$ is different across each slice, but is the same within each slice. Each slice will require it's own calculation for weight, $l$ and $\sigma'\tan \phi$

To find $\sigma'$ we have to use the force normal to the failure slope angle, and then divide it by the area it is effecting, so in this case,
$$
\begin{align}
N=W\cos \alpha \\
\sigma'=\frac{N}{l\times 1} =\frac{W\cos \alpha}{l} \\
\tau_{f}=c'+\frac{W\cos \alpha}{l}\times \tan \phi  \\ \\

\sum M_{R}=\sum c_{i}l_{i}R+\sum W_{i}\cos \alpha_{i} \tan \phi_{i} R \\ \\

\sum M_{R}=\left[ \sum c_{i}l_{i}+\sum W_{i}\cos \alpha_{i} \tan \phi_{i}  \right] R
\end{align}

$$
Note that he slices on the left side of the Arc center will give us a **negative** x, our $M_{D}$ is the same as before, 
$$
M_{D}=\sum W_{i}x_{i}
$$
Important to note, when solving, we may not need to measure the angle $\alpha$ if the given $\phi$ is 0. 

When doing this problem, we can also simplify the $\sum c_{i}l_{i}$ is by taking a large section of the soil with that same $c$, and then solving it like a swedish cirlc.e

Due to water pressure "lifting" up the soil, we have to subtract it from each slice. To calculate $u_{i}$ , which is the water pressure,

u_{i} = h_{w}\times \gamma_{w}
$$
which is the height starting from the bottom middle of the segment to the top of the water.

When considering tension crack, we have to consider a line $Z_{cr}$ 
There are two kinds, dry tension cracks and tension cracks filled with water

for dry tension cracks, these basically "decapitate" or slice the top of the soil slope, making the top basically useless when it comes to supporting any kind of shear. $Z_{cr}$ is how deep the slice will go. 
$$
\sigma_{h}=k_{a}\sigma_{v}-2c\sqrt{ k_{a} }
$$
where $k_{a}$ is 1 for clays. 
$$
Z_{cr}=\frac{2c}{\sigma}
$$
where c is the shear strength or $S_{u}$
