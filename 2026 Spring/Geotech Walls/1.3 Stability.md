There are three types of main slope failures, 
* Translational Slope failure (infinite slope)
* Rotational Slope Failure
* Block Slope Failure
![[Pasted image 20260205142409.png]]


![[1.3 Infinite Slope|700]]

The equations that relate to this are 
$$
\begin{align}
FS=\frac{c'}{\gamma z\cos \beta \sin \beta}+\frac{\tan \phi'}{\tan \beta}
\end{align}
$$

Where $c'$ is cohesion, and $\gamma$ is the specific weight

if the cohesion is 0, then the equation entirely relies on the right side. (if the slope is steeper, FS will be less, if the slope is milder, the FS will be more)

The angle of repose is the angle at which a pile of soil can stand at before it "gives"
for example, sand goes down through a funnel, and the sand makes a small pile. As the sand piles up on the ground, it makes a conical pyramid. The horizontal angle the pyramid has stays constant, even as the sand piles up. The sand just slides down. This angle is the **angle of repose**, also known as $\phi$
![[1.3 Sand Example|200]]
for a **fully submerged slope** we must use a different $\gamma$ because of the water
$$
\begin{align}
FS=\frac{c'}{\gamma' z\cos \beta \sin \beta}+\frac{\tan \phi'}{\tan \beta}
\end{align}
$$

### Rotational slope analysis

In this case the failure moment is in an arc. We need to find the Factor of Safety for one failure surface. 
$$
FS=\frac{\sum\text{Resisting Moment}}{\sum\text{Driving Moment}}=\frac{\sum M_{R}}{\sum M_{D}}
$$
To put it simply, $M_{R}$ is how much the soil can resist while $M_{D}$ is the force acting on the soil. In this case, The only force acting on the soil is the **weight of the soil** itself. 
![[Pasted image 20260210141220.png]]

To simplify doing this, we can cut the slope into smaller easier shapes. 

Drawing a line vertically down from the center of the arc, we get $x$. If we multiply this $x$ with $W$, the weight of the soil, this gives us the $M_{D}$ of the soil, or the force acting on the soil.
$$
\sum M_{D}=W_{1}x_{1}
$$

To calculate $M_{R}$ or the resistance of the soil, we get
$$
M_{R}=clR
$$
in the end, our equation for the factor of safety is
$$
FS=\frac{\sum c_{i}l_{i}R}{\sum W_{i}x_{i}}
$$
