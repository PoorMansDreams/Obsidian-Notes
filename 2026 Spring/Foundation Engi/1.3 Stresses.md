Stresses in a soil comes from two things
* Stress from geostatic stresses (stress from self weight)
* Induced Stress (stress from a load like a house)
The deeper you go, the more stress the soil experiences.

![[Foundation  Ex1]]
There are two stresses, total stress and effective stress, $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$, we will be using effective stress, which is the true stress. Total stress ignores the weight of water. Effective stress worries about the weight change due to water.

note that $\sigma$ is the total stress, it ignores the water's "strength", and only assumes the weight of the soil
$u$ is the water, the specific weight of the water is the same across the entire slope,
$\sigma'=\sigma-u$, using this formula, we basically subtract the specific weight of whatever is below the water line by the specific weight of water, ex, $2(112-62.4)$. 

The "slope" of a the line tells use how much stress there is per 1 unit, in this case, the "run" is the specific weight for every "rise" (the height or depth of the soil)
