
Factors to consider in selecting and designing the
type of foundation system for a building:

• Pattern and magnitude of building loads
• Subsurface and groundwater conditions
• Topography of the site
• Impact on adjacent properties
• Building code requirements
• Construction method and risk

Board Pile (EU) = Drilled Shafts (US)

Driven usually means it is hit by a hammer (works like nails)
	Could also be jacked (pushed slowly), uncommon

Drilled Shafts are drilled, after the hole is made we fill it up with concrete. (Concrete Pillar)

Steel H-piles
	It is steel beam that is used as a column instead. These steel columns will be driven without pre-drilling. There is low amount of displacement because the steel is quite thin. There is a limitation, to transport the Steel H beams. Transportation of the H-beams requires oversized trailers, And these oversized trailers can only get so long before it simply wont be able to make turns on the road. 
	![[Pasted image 20260407155934.png]]
	To get longer H-piles, we do splices. (weld the H-beams together or bolting)
	Pile = the column that's under the building. 
	Pile caps are designed like a footing, but the support reaction is from the piles (deep foundation)
	Pile caps are used to safely distribute the reaction force from the piles to the building.
	There are often studs that helps connect the pile cap to the pile. 
	It is important to monitor if the steel H piles as it is being hammers, (to see if it is actually being driven or if it is stuck)

Steel Pipe Piles
	If the pipe is hollow, then it is a low displacement pile. But if the bottom bit is covered, more soil will be displaced. Therefore it will be a high displacement pile, but it will also be stronger due to the displaced soil being more condensed, giving a stronger hugging effect.

Soil must be some cohesive stiff clay/soil so that the hole doesn't close up from caving. There might be a moment load or tension on the top half of the concrete, so we'll need some steel reinforcement. 

We cannot drop the concrete into the hole directly, we must pour it near the surface of the concrete. We use a tremie which is a floppy long funnel. If we drop the concrete from the top of the hole, it will cause the heavier aggregates to fall to the bottom, while the fines will rise to the top. We do not want aggregates to separate. 

![[Pasted image 20260407163053.png]]

If the soil is prone to caving, we simply drive in a steel pipe that will prevent caving in soil. We then take out the soil inside the pipe. We then fill the casing (steel pipe) with concrete. 

We can re-use/harvest the casing by pouring the concrete and lifting the casing such that the surface of the concrete being poured is higher than the depth of the pipe. We can't take the casing out after the concrete is poured because it will be too difficult. 

![[Pasted image 20260407163940.png]]
What we can also do is use a rotary mud wash to drill into the soil using drilling mud. The drilling mud will fill the hole, but also keep it from collapsing/caving. 

After the hole is dug out and filled with drilling mud, we inject concrete from the bottom of the hole using a tremie. The concrete is heavier than the drilling mud, so it will slowly force the drilling mud out of the hole. Eventually the concrete will replace the mud completely. 

![[Pasted image 20260407163645.png]]

We can have a head that can opens outwards once we reach a desirable depth. This creates a conical shape at the bottom of the hole. When we fill it up with concrete it creates a "shoe". Like a footing, improves capabilities.

![[Pasted image 20260407164113.png]]

We can combine drilling and concrete filling at the same time. The drill rotates to reach the bottom of the soil, then once we reached a desirable depth. We STOP rotating, and slowly lift the drill up. We don't rotate that auger because it will mix the soil cuttings with the concrete. As the auger is lifted, the pipe in the middle of the auger will shoot concrete deep into the soil. Since this is an enclosed space, we can force the concrete outwards into the soil, which gives a rugged texture and a better hugging effect. 

![[Pasted image 20260407164449.png]]

We can also use a smaller diameter tip and then have it be pushed apart by a larger diameter shoulder, effectively making it act like a "driven" or medium/high displacement method.

![[Pasted image 20260407164715.png]]

Franki piles are similar to drilling with casings, but while pouring the concrete, we smash a weight into the casing/steel pipe to force the concrete outwards to dig into the soil.
