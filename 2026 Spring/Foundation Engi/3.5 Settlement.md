Settlement is also the compression of the soil from several different factors, this could be
- Static loads
- Dynamic Loads
- Groundwater Drop (water stops supporting the soil)
- Loads from adjacent structures

![[Pasted image 20260317160021.png]]

The compression of soil can be seen as 
$$
\delta=\int ^{z_{\infty}} _{z_{0}}\epsilon_{z}dz
$$
![[Pasted image 20260317160240.png]]

Just like steel, wood and any other material, soil is a material. It has a $E$, modulus of elasticity. 
$$
E=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_{z}}
$$
In this case, the change in size due to the pressure is unconstrained and the material and blow "outwards"

however, in this case we need constrained modulus. This just means that we restrain the soil laterally, so it can only be squished vertically.
$$
M=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_{z}}=\frac{1}{m_{v}}
$$
in a laterally constrained condition,
$\epsilon_{r}=0$
$\epsilon_{z}=\epsilon_{vol}=\frac{\Delta V}{V}=\frac{\Delta e}{1+e_{0}}$
therefore, 
$$
m_{v}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{z}}\cdot \frac{\Delta e}{1+e_{0}}
$$

![[Pasted image 20260317161730.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260317161743.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260317161801.png]]

$$
S=\delta=m_{v}\cdot \Delta \sigma \cdot H
$$
This is the simplified equation, where
$m_{v}=\frac{1}{M}$
$\Delta \sigma$ is the induced stress at the center of the layer undergoing compression
$H$ is the thickness of the layer undergoing compression

If the layer is too think, and the approximation is too aggressive, we can cut the layer into sublayers. 

![[Pasted image 20260317162319.png]]

Doing this will increase the accuracy, and slightly less settlement. 

another method we could solve is the $e$-log $\sigma'$ method or e-log $p$  method. This method uses the recompression index, and the compression index. 

![[Pasted image 20260317162605.png]]


![[Pasted image 20260317162811.png]]

to use this method, we similarly use the same equation for our modulus method, but with a tweak
$$
\delta=\epsilon_{z}\times H
$$
we can also do sub layers with this method as well.
