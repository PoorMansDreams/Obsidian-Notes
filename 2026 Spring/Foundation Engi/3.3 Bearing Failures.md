Soil is unable to bear weight, and "gives". 
The active soil wedge is directly below the footing, footing will push the soil underneath
The soil will push laterally due to the horizontal force. The soil "gets out of the way". This causes the Soil in the active zone to push against soil in the transition zones. These transition zones will be pushed against the passive zones. These zones only develop due to the footing force placed on then. The passive zones may heave or rise up due to the force by the footing. 

Between each zone, there are shear planes on each. The shear resistance of the soil matters greatly when it comes to analyzing the soil.

To calculate for the bearing capacity, we can use **Terzaghi's**
$$
\begin{align} q_u &= c'N_c + \sigma'_{v,D}N_q + 0.5\gamma'BN_\gamma && \text{(continuous or strip foundation)} \\ q_u &= 1.3c'N_c + \sigma'_{v,D}N_q + 0.4\gamma'BN_\gamma && \text{(square foundation)} \\ q_u &= 1.3c'N_c + \sigma'_{v,D}N_q + 0.3\gamma'BN_\gamma && \text{(circular foundation)} \end{align}
$$
The $c'$ term describes the friction of the soil.

The $\sigma'_{D}$ describes the force from the soil above the passive zones pressing down against it, keeping it down.

The $\gamma'$ terms describes how heavy the soil is, heavier soil means it is stronger, since it is harder to move the soil. 

Every term actually also has a $N$ term. This $N$ term is a function of $\phi$, the **friction angle**. Every single term is altered by the friction angle. 

There is also a a $S$ term that applies to all them, we only use when we have a 3D shape. 
This is because Terzaghi's equation only sees the footing as 2D, meaning the footing extends infinitely into the page.
