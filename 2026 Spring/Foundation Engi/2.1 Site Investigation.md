A few methods to drill or dig through soil.

Solid-Stem

We put a long stick with a giant screw with a drill bit at the end and drill it into the earth. As the drill goes deeper into the earth, we can put "dead sticks" or just rods in place of the screws so we can reach deeper into the earth. Issue with this drilling method is that **sampling is difficult**, this is because the hole you make can collapse, and realistically, the hole will only stay open if the soil is clayey.

Hollow-Stem continuous Flight auger
* can sample and drill at the same time
* The auger and drill string advance together
* at the desired depth, the drill string can be removed for sampling
* augers hold borehole open, allows sampling cut bottom
* used in clays and granular soils above water
* below groundwater, soils can flow or heave into augers
* common to switch to mud-rotary below groundwater table
* Outer diameter (cutting) ~ 2 times inner diameter (sampler)
* Will need to be bigger, ex, a 3 inch sample will require a 6-7 inch borehole. 
	* Things to note, as the drill goes down into the soil, the water will want to go up into the drill. 
	* Past the water table line, the water causes the soil to want to go up into the drill hole. 
	* This causes the soil to break apart. This is called **heaving** or **boiling**
	* To know if heaving is occuring or not, we can use a measuring tape.
	* If the distance measured is less than how much we actually drilled, then we know that heaving is occurring

Rotary-Wash/Mud-Rotary
* This method uses a nozzle that pumps "drill mud" into the bottom of the borehole
* This drill mud is very hydrophilic, it absorbs ALOT of water. The unit weight of this mud will be greater than the soil
* As we cut through the soil, the mud has a higher unit weight, so the soil will actually float to the top of the drill mud
* The Mud "pushes" against the pore water pressure
* Side discharge bits are preferred when sampling is desired
* Discharged drill fluid is left to settle and repumped
* Drill string removed and bit replaced with sampler
* Mud should remain on the top of boring (if not, indication of a permeable layer in the boring)

Sonic
* Combined rotary-vibrating drilling
* Continuous coring/sampling possible
* Double Cased 

Disturbed vs Undisturbed
* Distrubed samples can be used for
	* Stratification
	* gradation
	* Atterberg Limits
	* Water Content
	* Compaction
* Undistrubed samples can be used for
	* Strength
	* Permeability
	* Compressibility/consolidation
	* Density
	* Stiffness
* Very difficult to obtain undisturbed samples for granular soils, can be sampled by freezing or gel-push)

There are several types of Samplers for soil

* Split Spoon Sampler
	* Disturbed samples
	* Used in SPT
	* Driven into the soil 
	* Standard size: 24" long, 2" Outer diameter, 1.375" Inner diameter
		* Area ratio is $\frac{OD^2-ID^2}{ID^2}\times 100\%=112\%$ 
	* Used in sands, silts, clays, but not good in gravels
	* The disturbance that can happen during this sampling process happens when the steel sides break or push apart the steel 
	* 10% is the maximum ratio
* Thin-Wall/Shelby Tube sampler
	* Undisturbed samples for cohesive soils
	* Most common size: 30" long, 3" Outer diameter, 2.875", Inner diameter 
		* Area Ratio is $\frac{OD^2-ID^2}{ID^2}\times 100\%=9\%$
	* Larger diameter is possible
	* Pushed not driven (not striked down)
	* Push, wait at least 10 min, rotate twice, pull
	* Take water content samples form ends, do torvane and pocket penetrometer tests
	* Seal ends with wax or O-ring packer, label top and bottom
