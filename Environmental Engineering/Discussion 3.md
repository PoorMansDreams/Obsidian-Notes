Difference between Toxicant and Toxin

|Feature|Toxicant|Toxin|
|---|---|---|
|**Definition**|Any poisonous substance that can cause harm.|A toxicant that is specifically produced by a living organism.|
|**Origin**|**Non-biological** (man-made, synthetic, or mineral/elemental).|**Biological** (produced by plants, animals, bacteria, or fungi).|
|**Examples**|Lead, carbon monoxide, $\text{PCB}$s, pesticides, industrial solvents.|Snake venom, botulinum toxin, ricin, mycotoxins.|
|**Classification**|The **general** category of poisonous agents.|A **specific type** of toxicant.|

Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances, these substances stay forever in the environment, which means they will eventually end in up whatever we eat and drink. They are "forever" chemicals.

Some examples of PFAS

- **Non-stick coatings** (like Teflon cookware).
    
- **Water- and stain-resistant coatings** for fabrics, carpets, and clothing.
    
- **Firefighting foams** (Aqueous Film-Forming Foam - AFFF).
    
- **Food packaging** (grease-resistant paper).
    
- **Cosmetics** and personal care products.

Canned foods have been found with **Polychlorinated Biphenyls** or PCBs these usually form in fatty animals, and fish.

Thermal paper also has a lot of BPA in them. 

PFAS is mainly in water, it isn't really a problem in the air.
