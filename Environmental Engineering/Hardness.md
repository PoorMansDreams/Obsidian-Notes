Water hardness can be defined as the sum of [[normalities]] of "all" multivalent cations (charges of +2 or higher)

Total Hardness (**TH**): Ca$^{2+}$ , Mg$^{2+}$, Al$^{3+}$
Carbonate Hardness (**CH**): CO$_{3}^{2-}$ (alkalinity)

Hardwater has: High TH, CH, pH
Softwater has: law TH, CH, pH

###### Hardness Example

Compute the hardness (TH), non-carbonated hardness (NCH) and carbonate hardness (CH) of the sample or river water present below. Answers should be in $\frac{meq}{L}$

| Ion         | Symbol | River water (M) |
| ----------- | ------ | --------------- |
| Sodium      | Na+    | 0.23e-3         |
| Magnesium   | Mg²⁺   | 0.15e-3         |
| Calcium     | Ca²⁺   | 0.33e-3         |
| Potassium   | K+     | 0.03e-3         |
| Chloride    | C1-    | 0.16e-3         |
| Sulfate     | SO4²-  | 0.07e-3         |
| Bicarbonate | HCO3-  | 0.86e-3         |
To compute total hardness (**TH**), we add up all the +2 charges, in this case, Mg and Ca, the molarity is already given, so 
$$
TH=(0.15\times 10^{-3}\times 2)+(0.33\times 10^{-3}\times 2)=0.00096 \frac{eq}{L}
$$
$0.15 \times 10^{-3}$ Molarity times 2, which is the charge. Then we add this to the other ion, Ca
We then convert $\frac{eq}{L}$ to $\frac{meq}{L}$
$$
0.00096 \frac{eq}{L} = 0.96 \frac{meq}{L}
$$

To calculate the carbonate hardness, we take all the **Nc** which is the normalities of the carbonate species. In this case, the only carbonate is the bicarbonate
$$
Nc=0.86\times 10^{-3}\times 1=0.00086 \frac{eq}{L}=0.86 \frac{meq}{L}
$$
To determine CH, NCH, we have to find TH and Nc first. 
If Nc < TH, CH=Nc, NCH=TH-CH
If Nc > TH, CH=TH, NCH=0

So in this case, **TH > Nc**, so CH = $0.86 \frac{meq}{L}$ , and NCH=$0.96-0.86=0.1 \frac{meq}{L}$
