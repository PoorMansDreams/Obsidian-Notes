For the reaction of HA, an acid and water, which in this case is the base,
$$
\text{HA}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{aq}) + \text{A}^-(\text{aq})
$$
We see that the equilibrium constant will give us 
$$
K_{\text{a}} = \frac{[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+][\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]} = \frac{[\text{H}^+][\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}
$$
a large $K_a$ means strong acid, while a small $K_a$ means weaker acid.

| Property                                                                   | Strong acid                    | Weak acid                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Ka value                                                                   | Kₐ is large (typically Kₐ≥1)   | Ka is small                      |
| Equilibrium concentration of H+ compared with original concentration of HA | [H⁺] ≈ [HA]₀                   | [H⁺] « [HA]₀                     |
| Strength of conjugate base compared with that of water                     | A- much weaker base than water | A- much stronger base than water |

$pH=-\log[H+]$
$pOH=-\log[OH-]$
$pH+pOH=14$

for a 0.1 M HNO$_3$

$$
pH=-\log[0.1]=1
$$

For 0.1 M of F- follows below dissociation in water with $K_b$ of $1.4 \cdot 10^{-11}$

| $H_{2}O$        | $F^-$ | $HF$ | $OH^-$ |
| --------------- | ----- | ---- | ------ |
| Initial         | 0.1   | 0    | 0      |
| **Change**      | -x    | +x   | +x     |
| **Equilibrium** | 0.1-x | x    | x      |


$$
H_{2}O+F^-\rightleftharpoons HF+OH^-
$$
$$
K_{b}=\frac{[HF][OH^-]}{[F^-]}=\frac{[x][x]}{0.1-x}=\frac{x^2}{0.1-x}=1.4\cdot 10^{-11}
$$
$$
x=1.18\cdot 10^{-6}
$$
the change in OH is x, so then we can calculate that

$$
pOH=-\log[OH^-]=-\log[1.18\cdot 10^{-6}]=5.928
$$
which means that 
$$
pH=14-5.928=8.07
$$
